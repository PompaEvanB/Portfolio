Evocation

Project Role: Game Designer / Level Designer

Initial Ideation

Initially, the started off as a puzzle game where the player could use a ball to change their surroundings. Like a forcefield that would either reveal the past or present. It could be thrown or dropped to keep some puzzle elements active while you were away from it. Eventually, we wanted to increase the pace of the game, so we made it a platformer. Creatively revealing the past was the core of our game, so we decided to let players be as creative as they want. Removing entire pieces of the level if they wanted to, the execution of these actions became the puzzle we were designing.

Mechanics Design

In the final game, the ball that reveals the past is more like a spotlight that is controlled with the mouse. Removing geometry in the world the player is in or revealing geometry for them to use. Mapping the spotlight to the mouse made the most sense because we wanted the player to move it at whatever speed they were comfortable with.

Level Design

In the first section of the game. The player is forced to use their ability to proceed. The solution is straight forward, they must use the spotlight to create a bridge using hidden geometry. Since this is at the very start of the level, failure has little consequence. As the game goes on, the challenges become more nonlinear. The best example is at this intersection where the player has to reveal platforms to move upward. But they don't have to follow the intended path. Instead, they can make a hole in the far-right wall and skip the section entirely.

Sledge

Project Role: Game Designer / Level Designer / programmer

Game Concept

Use a hammer to rapidly propel yourself.

Initial Ideation

The core of this game is the hammer. But how the hammer was going to be used was up in the air. We wanted this game to be all about movement, so initially the player didn't have a jump. This was so players would focus more on the hammer itself. The problem was players found it difficult to make small hammer bounces, so we added a small jump. We also weren't sure what we wanted the format of the game to be. There were initial talks of it being open world or more of an exploration game. But eventually we landed on level-based game play with some exploration. Kind of like classic 2D sonic games.

Mechanics Design

The mechanics of the hammer are very similar to how rocket jumping works in most games. By angling the hammer at the ground behind you, the force pushes you in the opposite direction. These can be chained together to get to high speeds. There's also a parry/light swing. This is for parrying enemy projectiles or for hitting an enemy without sending yourself flying.

Level Design

So far, I have made levels one, three, and four. Each level serves a purpose and has a theme.

Level One: Basics

The whole point of level one is to teach players how to play the game. The first few challenges can be done without the hammer, and eventually indicators are placed in the level for where to use the hammer. Ending the level with a big hamer bounce off of a wall. The first instance where you have to chain two bounces together.

Level Three: Walls

Level three is all about using walls to gain speed or height. Several big walls are used throughout the level to help players get to the next area, and basically every wall can be used in this way.

Level Four: Intertwining paths

This level is technically incomplete, but a lot of it has been revised. This level has two main paths, and switching between these paths is the key to success. Each path by themselves is long, but when you swap between them, you can beat the level fairly quickly.

Timeline

Project Role: Sole Creator

Game Concept

Create a 2d platformer where the player uses their movements in the past to solve puzzles in the present.

Initial Ideation

The initial ideation of this game did not change much from the final design given that I had two days to make the game. I wanted to make a game where the players past actions could affect the current situation they were in. Having the player use buttons or levers in the past seemed like a direct way they could interact with objects like doors or platforms.

Mechanics Design

The player can start recording their actions with the "F" key. To play their actions again, press "G". To cancel press "R". When the recording starts, a clone of the player will be dropped where the player was standing. It cannot be interacted with. Once the player presses play, the clone will do all of the actions you just did at the same speed. This lets your past actions help you progress through the level.

Level Design

The level design is a series of small puzzles to showcase the main mechanic. For example, the first challenge require the player to record themselves on a button to get through a locked door.

Final Thoughts

This is one of the games I think of the most. I really love the concept, but it is really rough around the edges. It was one of my first games ever, and I didn't have a lot of time to make it.
